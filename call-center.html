PROJECT 03 // INTRO TO AI · NOVEMBER 2026

Good engineering.
Terrible customer service.

The AI Call Center Lab—also known as Barely Functional Call Center—uses intentionally unhelpful fictional personalities to explore a serious engineering problem: making a voice pipeline work consistently enough to demonstrate, measure, and improve.

READ EVIDENCE LOG ↗DOWNLOAD PDF ↓

One controller, three characters

The supplied evidence log records 40 numbered missions, beginning with a single voice assistant and ending with three distinct characters: Karen, Chad, and Priya. A shared Python session loop coordinates push-to-talk audio, transcription, model requests, dialogue validation, optional speech preparation, and synchronous playback.

  1. 01 / LISTENPush-to-talk microphone
  2. 02 / TRANSCRIBELocal faster-whisper
  3. 03 / GENERATEExternal LLM via OpenRouter
  4. 04 / VALIDATEDialogue rules and optional local preparation
  5. 05 / SYNTHESIZEPiper through ComfyUI
  6. 06 / REVIEWWAV playback and OBS recording

Audio processing stays local in the documented pipeline; recognized text is sent to the external model. This is a classroom prototype, not a production customer-service platform.

Measure before rewriting

A provider latency spike pushed time to first token from a reported 0.14–0.54 seconds to 23.4–31.8 seconds. Comparing controller timings with provider metadata pointed to external queueing. A different model showed much faster responses in bounded tests, but introduced new punctuation and pacing problems.

The useful result was the diagnosis. Transcription, generation, synthesis, and playback were measured separately so one slow stage did not trigger an unnecessary rewrite of the whole system.

Listening changed the implementation

Prompt-only sentence rules were unreliable. A conservative local speech-preparation step was tested instead. The evidence log reports that 19 of 19 offline cases preserved the original words, while an A/B listening example increased observed pauses from two to four.

The feature was enabled for Karen after listening approval. It stayed off for Chad after a comma changed an idiom’s meaning, and off for Priya when the prepared output was identical. Different characters needed different settings.

What the record demonstrates

  • Integration across local speech tools and an external language-model service.
  • Versioned configurations and reversible experiments.
  • Explicit records of rejected replies, provider failures, and unsuccessful prompts.
  • Human listening decisions alongside automated validation.

The log combines selected primary mission records with later completion summaries. Its timings and pass counts describe specific tests, not controlled benchmarks or guaranteed reliability.
